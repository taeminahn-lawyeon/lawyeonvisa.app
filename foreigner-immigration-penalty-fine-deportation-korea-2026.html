Immigration Penalty Notices and Stay Restrictions for Foreigners in Korea

Category Criminal · Violation ReviewReading time approx. 7 min

Paying an immigration penalty does not itself secure permission to remain in Korea. The same violation can lead to a penalty notice and restrictions on residence, and may also proceed through the criminal justice system. The alleged violation, the effect of payment and the right to continue living in Korea are separate questions.

An immigration penalty case involves more than the amount stated in the notice. For someone maintaining a family or business in Korea, the consequences also include permission to stay, a possible removal measure and restrictions on returning after departure. Each decision has a different basis and effect.

Penalty notices, criminal fines and administrative fines

An immigration penalty, or beomchikgeum, is an amount notified for payment through the immigration penalty-notice procedure. Immigration authorities use this procedure to deal with violations of the Immigration Control Act. It is distinct from a criminal fine imposed by a court, but it is not simply a fee unrelated to criminal enforcement.

A criminal fine is a punishment imposed by a court judgment or summary order. An administrative fine, or gwataeryo, is a separate sanction for an administrative violation. Not every failure to meet a reporting obligation results in beomchikgeum; the type of sanction depends on the obligation and the applicable provision.

TypeHow it is imposedRelevance to residence
Immigration penalty: beomchikgeumPenalty notice issued by immigration authoritiesCriteria concerning immigration penalty records apply.
Criminal fineCourt judgment or summary orderCriteria for foreign nationals with final criminal fines may apply.
Administrative fine: gwataeryoAdministrative sanctionIt is not counted as an immigration penalty or criminal fine.

An immigration penalty of KRW 5 million and a criminal fine of KRW 5 million therefore cannot be treated as the same sanction. Nor can the two amounts simply be added together to determine whether removal follows.

What payment resolves and what remains

The statutory payment period for an immigration penalty notice is 15 days from receipt. Nonpayment generally leads to referral for criminal prosecution, subject to statutory exceptions, including payment before referral and issuance of a deportation order. Nonpayment does not automatically produce a final criminal fine.

Even where payment resolves the handling of the particular violation, it does not also grant an extension or change of status. Restrictions on permission to stay or removal measures may arise separately from the penalty notice.

If a departure order has already been issued, the payment deadline and the deadline for departure are separate. Payment does not itself extend the departure deadline or cancel the order.

Reading the amount and time-period criteria

The integrated stay-permission restriction criteria cited in the residence manual include an immigration penalty of at least KRW 5 million for a first offence and cumulative penalties of at least KRW 7 million within the preceding three years for repeat violations. These differ in both amount and period from the criminal-fine criterion of KRW 5 million within five years.

These figures alone do not determine every application for residence permission or the type of removal measure. The criteria for the particular permission sought, the nature and repetition of the violation, and applicable exceptions also matter. A restriction on acquiring a particular status is not the same decision as removal from current residence.

Being below a monetary threshold does not guarantee permission to stay. The underlying violation may independently raise a residence restriction or removal issue.

The alleged violation and the need to remain

Where unauthorized employment or another activity is alleged, the actual duties, activity period, scope of permission and whether a violation occurred are substantive issues. Disputing the violation and explaining why residence is necessary even if a violation is established address different decisions.

Family relationships, child care, treatment and business operations have significance in relation to applicable exception criteria. The existence of a family or business does not exempt a person from every restriction. The circumstances also need to explain why that person must remain in Korea.

Lawyeon compares the alleged violation with the applicable criteria and develops the arguments and evidence concerning residence permission. This connects the response to the monetary penalty with the work required to maintain life in Korea.

Responding to a removal measure

A refusal of residence permission, a departure order and a deportation order are different decisions with different procedures. The Immigration Control Act objection against a deportation order is filed within seven days of receiving the order. That period does not apply in the same way to a departure order or refusal of residence permission.

An action to revoke a decision and an application to suspend its effect or enforcement have their own requirements. Filing a lawsuit does not itself suspend the obligation to depart or enforcement. The type of notice, receipt date and departure deadline determine the response timetable.

Where an entry ban is also imposed, the results of removal proceedings and ban relief remain distinct. An entry restriction may remain after the residence issue is addressed. Conversely, an exemption from an entry ban does not remove the obligation to leave if a departure order remains in force.
