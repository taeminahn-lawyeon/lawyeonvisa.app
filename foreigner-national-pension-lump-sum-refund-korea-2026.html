Korean National Pension refunds for E-9 and H-2 workers

Category Immigration AdministrationReading time approx. 6 min

Contents

Foreign nationals who contributed to Korea’s National Pension under E-9 or H-2 status can receive their contributions plus interest as a lump-sum refund when returning to their home country. Those who have already left Korea can claim through a representative and receive payment into an overseas account.

Eligibility for a lump-sum refund

For foreign nationals, eligibility generally depends on whether their home country provides an equivalent benefit to Korean nationals or whether a social security agreement with Korea provides for lump-sum refunds.

A special rule allows refunds regardless of nationality for people covered under E-9, H-2 or the former trainee-employment E-8 status. The current seasonal-work E-8 status is different from the former trainee-employment category and is not covered by this special rule.

Contributions and the refund amount

The refund includes both the worker’s and the employer’s contributions, plus interest. In 2026, workplace-based contributions total 9.5% of standard monthly income, shared equally between the worker and employer at 4.75% each.

The total refund is based on contributions actually paid during each period of coverage. Earlier contributions were calculated at the rates in force at the time, so multiplying today’s monthly contribution by the total number of months worked may give an incorrect figure. The National Pension Service’s records establish the contributions paid and the interest due.

Overseas claims and the limitation period

A refund can be claimed before departure or after returning home. A person who cannot readily travel back to Korea can appoint a representative to file the claim and receive payment into an overseas account.

A refund entitlement arising from a return to the home country expires if it is not claimed within five years of the entitlement arising. An overseas claim requires time for authentication and delivery of original documents, which must be allowed for when calculating the time remaining before the deadline.

Changes in immigration status and contribution history

For someone who worked under E-9, changed status and later left Korea, their final status alone may not determine refund eligibility for earlier coverage periods. Comparing their immigration history with National Pension records identifies the periods covered by the special rule; reciprocity or a social security agreement may also apply.

Where a person has changed employers, coverage periods and contributions paid by each workplace also need to be checked. For an overseas claim, these records establish the scope of the application. The representative can then confirm the National Pension Service’s requirements for authorisation and bank-account evidence, including authentication and delivery.
