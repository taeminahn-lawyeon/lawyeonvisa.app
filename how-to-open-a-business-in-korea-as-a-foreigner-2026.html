Starting a business in Korea and applying for a D-9 visa

Updated 2026.04Reading time approx. 8 min

Contents

A D-9-4 or D-9-5 application is reviewed after substantial completion of the Korean business preparations, including the lease, registration and industry licensing. Applicants preparing from abroad therefore enter contracts and spend investment funds before the visa application. Contract terms and the application schedule need to be planned when selecting the business. The examples below are hypothetical.

Korean residence experience and business selection

Food-service businesses require Korean for customer service and supplier management, as well as familiarity with local commercial practices. Applicants who have not lived in Korea may consider businesses requiring less direct conversation with customers. Kiosk-based and remotely managed shops place fewer demands on language skills. Convenience stores involve customer contact but offer standardized procedures and franchisor support.

Applicants who have studied or worked in Korea may also consider more customer-facing businesses, such as cafés or restaurants serving food from their home country. Graduates of Korean universities may qualify for D-9-5, depending on their degree and other eligibility requirements.

Residence experience and language skills affect which business the applicant can personally operate. Available funds determine the investment and initial operating budget. Family needs and the intended relocation date also affect the scale of the premises and the opening schedule.

Leasing, registration, licensing and the visa application

For a business requiring authorization, the business registration application must include the relevant permit, registration certificate or confirmation of notification. If registration is sought before authorization is obtained, a business plan or other permitted documentation can be submitted instead. The documents and processing time required for industry authorization also affect the visa application and entry schedule.

C-3-4 short-term business status covers activities such as market research, business discussions and contract signing. Operating the business itself requires an appropriate status of stay.

D-9-4 visa applications are generally made at a Korean mission in the applicant’s home country. Applicants covered by the manual’s exceptions, including long-term residence abroad, may apply at a mission in their country of residence. Foreigner registration must be completed within 90 days of entry on the visa.

Opening costs and contract and document risks

Spending the entire investment on franchise fees and facilities can leave the business without sufficient working capital. If it cannot continue operating while sales develop, it will also struggle to establish the operating record needed for an extension of stay.

A franchisor’s promotional claims about revenue and costs can be compared with its disclosure document, including its finances and closure rates. Signing a lease before franchise approval exposes the applicant to deposit and cancellation costs even if the franchise application fails.

Official documents obtained abroad, including criminal record certificates, can take considerable time to issue and authenticate. Waiting until Korean contracts and registrations are complete to begin obtaining them can delay the visa application.

Lease commitments during franchise screening

Suppose a franchisor is assessing an application for a particular location while the landlord demands an early lease commitment. Signing to secure the premises may leave the applicant with lease obligations but no planned business if franchise approval is refused.

The relevant questions are how far the franchise assessment has progressed and whether the lease’s special terms and termination provisions can accommodate its outcome. Signing also commits investment funds and affects the visa schedule, so the contracts must fit the overall relocation timetable.

In addition, we recommend obtaining a review of the overall structure before contracts are signed and funds begin to move. Frequently asked questions

Can I get the visa first and then prepare the business in Korea?
No. D-9-4 and D-9-5 applications are reviewed after substantial completion of business registration, licensing and the other Korean business preparations.
Is business immigration possible without Korean?
D-9-4 has no Korean-language requirement. The Korean needed for customer service and supplier management varies by business. Applicants who have not lived in Korea may consider businesses with less direct customer contact.
