Reapplication and appeals after a stay extension or status change denial

Category Criminal · Violation ReviewReading time approx. 7 min

After a stay extension or change of status is denied, the applicant needs to establish whether the missing requirements can be met through reapplication or whether to challenge the denial. An application that failed the income or employment requirements calls for different evidence from one denied because of past violations despite meeting those requirements. A denial issued in Korea can be challenged through an administrative appeal or an action for cancellation.

The departure deadline in a denial notice

The applicant must check the departure deadline stated in the denial notice and any remaining period of stay. The departure deadline and the deadlines for administrative appeals or court proceedings are calculated separately. Starting a challenge does not automatically extend the departure deadline or the permitted stay.

Remaining after the deadline creates an overstay and an additional risk of removal. The overstay record can also count against later applications.

Reasons for denial and information disclosure

Denial notices often give only a brief explanation. The specific reasons can be requested from the authority or obtained through an information disclosure request. Submitting the same documents without identifying the disputed requirement or record may leave the reason for refusal unresolved and add a repeat-application record.

If income, employment or supporting documents fell short of the requirements, reapplication depends on whether the shortfall can be remedied. If a criminal fine, immigration penalty or other violation caused the denial, the applicable rules and the circumstances of the violation provide the basis for assessing a legal challenge.

Reapplication and challenges to the denial

Applicants who did not meet an income or employment requirement can address the shortfall and reapply. Since the earlier application remains part of the record, the new documents should show what has changed or been supplemented. Inconsistent statements or resubmission of identical documents can count against the application.

Where a denial rests on past violations, or the requirements were met but the application was still refused, proving those requirements again may not address the reason for refusal. The lawfulness of the denial can be contested through an administrative appeal or an action for cancellation. An action for cancellation must be filed within 90 days of learning of the decision.

A refusal to extend or change immigration status issued in Korea can be challenged through an administrative appeal or an action for cancellation.

Proportionality of denials based on past violations

Where income and employment requirements were met but an extension was denied because of past immigration penalties, resubmitting income evidence or the employment contract does not address that history. A challenge can instead examine whether refusing further stay is excessive in light of the nature and circumstances of the violation.

The circumstances and repetition of a violation inform its effect on immigration control. Length of stay, settlement and family relationships in Korea explain the harm a denial would cause to the applicant’s life. Consistency of statements and compliance with procedures are also considered. Evidence of these facts can support an argument that the harm caused by refusing stay is disproportionate to the public interest served.

Appeal deadlines and stay during litigation

Alongside establishing the reasons for denial, the applicant must record when they learned of the decision, the departure deadline and the remaining period of stay. The departure deadline may arrive during litigation even if there was still time to file the action. Interim measures, including a stay of execution, must then be considered alongside the challenge to the denial.

Frequently asked questions

Can I litigate a denied extension?
A denial issued within Korea can be contested by administrative appeal or revocation suit, the latter within 90 days of learning of the disposition.
Can I just reapply with the same documents?
Submitting the same documents without addressing the reason for refusal can lead to another denial. Reapplication may be appropriate if a missing requirement can be met. A challenge may be appropriate if the authority applied the wrong criterion or incorrectly assessed the violation history.
