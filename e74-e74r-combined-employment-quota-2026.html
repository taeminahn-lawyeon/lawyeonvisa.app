E-7-4 and E-7-4R Employment Limits: When the General Rule Permits Additional Recommendations

Law Firm Lawyeon2026.09.18

Contents

1. Key Issues2. Implications

September 2026 · E-7-4 and E-7-4R Employment Allowance Rules

An employer with both E-7-4 skilled workers and E-7-4R regional skilled workers may be able to recommend additional workers even after reaching the cap in the regional employment table. The Immigration Residence Manual counts workers in both categories together but allows the general E-7-4 limit to apply when it is more favorable to the employer. This exception changes the employer’s recommendation capacity in bands where the regional cap remains fixed despite an increase in the qualifying Korean workforce.

1. Key Issues

(a) The combined-employment limit and the exception for the general rule

An employer using both E-7-4 and E-7-4R is generally subject to the regional employment allowance. Existing E-7-4 workers count toward that limit; the employer does not receive separate allowances for the two categories that can be added together. Employer recommendations must also remain within the business’s employment allowance.

The regional rules combine a percentage of the Korean workforce with a cap for the relevant headcount band. A business with 51 to 100 qualifying Korean employees may employ up to 50% of that number, subject to a maximum of 35 workers. With 80 qualifying Korean employees, calculating 50% does not, under the regional table alone, establish an allowance of 40.

Outside the construction sector, the general E-7-4 limit is ordinarily 30% of the qualifying Korean workforce, while a 50% rule applies to businesses in designated population-decline areas, root industries, agriculture, livestock farming and fisheries. The manual expressly provides for the more favorable general limit, citing, among other cases, such businesses with more than 71 qualifying Korean employees. This provision allows a business employing both categories to use the general rule and exceed the regional cap of 35 within the 51-to-100-employee band discussed above.

(b) Additional recommendations where a business has 80 Korean employees and 35 workers in the two categories

Consider a hypothetical manufacturer in a designated population-decline area with 80 Korean employees, 30 E-7-4 workers and 5 E-7-4R workers. Assume that all 80 Korean employees satisfy the minimum-wage requirement and have been enrolled in employment insurance for at least three months, meeting the headcount requirements.

CalculationResult
Regional rule80 × 50% = 40, reduced by the cap for this band → 35
General E-7-4 rule for a population-decline area80 × 50% → 40
Existing workers in both categories30 E-7-4 + 5 E-7-4R → 35
Remaining capacity under the general rule40 − 35 → 5

Under the regional table alone, the employer has exhausted its allowance. Applying the general rule for the population-decline area instead produces a total limit of 40, leaving headcount capacity for five additional recommendations. The result is neither 75 places obtained by adding the two limits nor 40 places for each category. It is a combined limit of 40 workers across both categories.

2. Implications

In this example, the employer can pursue five additional status changes without hiring more Korean employees or waiting for existing skilled workers to leave. Its location in a population-decline area and qualifying Korean headcount provide the basis for applying the general rule and making recommendations above the regional table’s cap. That calculation establishes capacity under the employer’s headcount requirement; an actual change of status also requires the worker to meet the relevant points, employment-contract and other eligibility requirements, as well as the local-government recommendation requirement for E-7-4R.

The workers’ existing status also affects the capacity used by the proposed changes. Moving an existing E-7-4 worker to E-7-4R leaves the combined total unchanged and does not create an additional place. An E-9 worker, by contrast, is excluded from this skilled-worker limit until changing to E-7-4 or E-7-4R. If the manufacturer changes five of its existing E-9 workers to either skilled-worker category, its overall workforce remains the same, but its combined skilled-worker headcount rises from 35 to 40. The five places available under the general rule can therefore support the transition of existing employees to a status that allows longer-term retention.
