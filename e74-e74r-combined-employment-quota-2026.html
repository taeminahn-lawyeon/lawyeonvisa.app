Exceptions to E-7-4 and E-7-4R employment limits

Law Firm Lawyeon2026.09.18

Contents

1. Key provisions2. Implications

September 2026 · E-7-4 and E-7-4R Employment Allowance Rules

An employer with both E-7-4 skilled workers and E-7-4R regional skilled workers may be able to recommend additional workers even after reaching the cap in the regional employment table. The Immigration Residence Manual counts workers in both categories together but allows the general E-7-4 limit to apply when it is more favorable to the employer. Where the regional cap stays fixed despite an increase in qualifying Korean employees, applying the general limit can make room for additional recommendations.

1. Key provisions

(a) Combined headcount and the general limit

An employer using both E-7-4 and E-7-4R is generally subject to the regional employment allowance. Existing E-7-4 and E-7-4R workers count toward a single combined limit. Employer recommendations must also remain within the business’s employment allowance.

The regional rules combine a percentage of the Korean workforce with a cap for the relevant headcount band. A business with 51 to 100 qualifying Korean employees may employ up to 50% of that number, subject to a maximum of 35 workers. With 80 qualifying Korean employees, the cap limits the allowance to 35, even though 50% of that workforce would be 40.

Outside the construction sector, the general E-7-4 limit is ordinarily 30% of the qualifying Korean workforce, while a 50% rule applies to businesses in designated population-decline areas, root industries, agriculture, livestock farming and fisheries. The manual expressly provides for the more favorable general limit, citing, among other cases, such businesses with more than 71 qualifying Korean employees. This provision allows a business employing both categories to use the general rule and exceed the regional cap of 35 within the 51-to-100-employee band discussed above.

(b) Additional places for an employer with 80 Korean employees

Suppose a manufacturer in a designated population-decline area employs 80 Korean employees, 30 E-7-4 workers and 5 E-7-4R workers. In this hypothetical example, all 80 Korean employees meet the minimum-wage requirement and have been enrolled in employment insurance for at least three months, so they qualify for the headcount calculation.

CalculationResult
Regional rule80 × 50% = 40, reduced by the cap for this band → 35
General E-7-4 rule for a population-decline area80 × 50% → 40
Existing workers in both categories30 E-7-4 + 5 E-7-4R → 35
Remaining capacity under the general rule40 − 35 → 5

The employer has reached the regional limit, but applying the general rule for the population-decline area increases its allowance to 40 and permits five additional recommendations. The 40-worker limit covers both categories together.

2. Implications

In this example, the employer can pursue five additional status changes without hiring more Korean employees or waiting for existing skilled workers to leave. Its location in a population-decline area and qualifying Korean headcount provide the basis for applying the general rule and making recommendations above the regional table’s cap. Even with those five additional places, each worker must meet the points, employment-contract and other eligibility requirements. A change to E-7-4R also requires a local-government recommendation.

Moving an existing E-7-4 worker to E-7-4R leaves the combined headcount unchanged, so it creates no additional place. An E-9 worker, by contrast, is excluded from this skilled-worker limit until changing to E-7-4 or E-7-4R. If the manufacturer changes five of its existing E-9 workers to either skilled-worker category, its overall workforce remains the same, but its combined skilled-worker headcount rises from 35 to 40. The five places available under the general rule can therefore be used to move existing E-9 employees into skilled-worker status and support their longer-term retention.
