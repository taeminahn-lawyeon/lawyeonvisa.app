Overview

The appropriate immigration status depends on the sponsoring organization, the form of contract and the source of income, even where the occupation or business plan appears similar. We assess whether the proposed activities are permitted and what will be needed for later extensions or changes of status.

Lawyeon handles visa applications, changes and extensions of status, professional employment, start-ups and foreign investment. Where several routes need to be considered, we compare their requirements and restrictions against the applicant's education and experience, business preparations and family plans.

Our Practice

Employment, job seeking and professional activities

We assess the status required for graduates seeking employment, professional workers, and people active in the arts and sports. Alongside the applicant's qualifications, we examine the employer or affiliated organization, actual duties, contract period and payment structure to align the application with the intended activity.

Where a contract is ending, a workplace change or multiple contracts are planned, or a client is moving into an independent business, we assess whether the current status can be maintained and what status is required for the new activity.

Our PracticeJob-seeking and employment visas · Professional, arts and sports activities · Workplace changes · Status changes and extensions

Business, start-ups and foreign investment

We advise on immigration requirements and the legal arrangements for operating a business in Korea, taking account of whether it is a sole proprietorship, corporate investment or technology start-up. Our review connects the business plan with the source and introduction of capital, premises, contracts and evidence of actual operations.

For clients considering start-up preparation followed by technology start-up status, we separately assess training, points and mandatory criteria, incorporation and business progress. Foreign investment reporting, capital remittance and establishment matters are reviewed against the investment structure and the nature of the funds.

Our PracticeBusiness and investment visas · Start-up preparation and technology start-ups · Foreign investment reporting · Business establishment advice

Family sponsorship and dependent entry

We review visas and sponsorship requirements for the spouse and minor children of a person residing in Korea. We assess how a marriage, parent-child relationship or guardianship established abroad is treated under the proposed status, and the official documents needed to demonstrate that relationship.

Where a case does not fit the usual family sponsorship requirements, we consider the relationship with the sponsor, the purpose of entry, and education and care plans to assess the available status. Any criminal or immigration history is also reviewed for its effect on eligibility and supporting documents.

Our PracticeSpouse and child sponsorship · Dependent entry visas · Overseas family relationships and guardianship · Advice on the purpose of entry
