Immigration detention and temporary release in Korea

Category Criminal · Violation ReviewReading time approx. 6 min

A person in immigration detention may obtain temporary release subject to a deposit, residence restrictions and other conditions. The authority considers flight risk and reasons for release, including treatment, family care and preparation for litigation.

Purpose and duration of immigration detention

Immigration detention secures a person’s presence for enforced removal. Although it is an administrative measure, restrictions on movement and communication can make it difficult for the detainee to gather evidence or prepare statements for litigation.

Following a deportation order, detention is initially limited to two months. Each extension requires prior approval from the Detention Committee and may be granted for up to three months. Total detention is generally capped at nine months, or 20 months for statutory exceptions. A detainee may challenge deportation by objection or lawsuit while detained; temporary release allows evidence gathering and preparation outside detention.

Temporary release requirements and deposits

Temporary release suspends confinement for a limited period without cancelling the detention decision. The Immigration Control Act allows a deposit of up to KRW 20 million, residence restrictions and other conditions, taking account of the detainee’s circumstances, reasons for release and assets.

The deposit reflects the person’s assets and the ability to secure attendance. A personal guarantor may also be required. Breaching release conditions may result in forfeiture of the deposit, immediate re-detention and an adverse assessment in later proceedings.

Eligible applicants and release criteria

The detainee, family members or counsel may apply for temporary release. Where the detainee cannot prepare the application directly, family and counsel gather the supporting materials outside detention.

The authority considers a fixed address and family ties in Korea, the medical needs of the detainee or family, pending litigation and preparation needs, and the ability to provide a deposit and guarantor. Supporting evidence should substantiate the reasons for release and address flight risk.

Medical needs and flight risk

A need for continuing treatment may not be enough to secure release if the detainee has no settled address in Korea. The application should explain the nature and urgency of treatment and how accommodation and attendance will be secured after release.

The stage of removal proceedings and the person’s history of attendance and compliance also inform the assessment. Medical evidence supports the need for release; accommodation and guarantee arrangements address flight risk.

Appeals and conditions during temporary release

Deadlines for an objection or lawsuit against the deportation order must be observed without waiting for a temporary-release decision. The deportation order remains in force after release, and the person must comply with reporting and residence conditions while pursuing the challenge.

Frequently asked questions

Does temporary release cancel the deportation?
No. Temporary release restores physical liberty for a limited time before removal is executed; the deportation itself must be contested separately by objection or revocation suit.
Who can file the request?
The detainee, family members and counsel may all file. Where the detainee cannot act from inside, it is common for family and counsel to prepare the materials and file from outside.
