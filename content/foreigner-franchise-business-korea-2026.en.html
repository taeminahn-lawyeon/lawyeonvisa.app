Investment fund requirements for D-9-4 and D-9-5 visas

The investment threshold is KRW 300 million for D-9-4 and KRW 100 million for D-9-5 (as of 2026). Immigration examines both the amount and how the funds were obtained, transferred to Korea and spent on the business. Funds may not qualify as investment if those steps cannot be documented, even when the total meets the threshold.

Foreign capital transfers and sources of funds

Investment funds obtained abroad must be brought into Korea in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act. Remittance, currency exchange and other records must establish how the funds entered Korea.

The declared purpose of the transfer must also be investment. Money brought in for living expenses or tuition can present problems if it is later used as business investment.

The applicant must document how the funds were obtained, whether from employment, business income, inheritance, a gift or an asset sale. Where a family member sent money on the applicant’s behalf, the applicant must also establish that the funds belong to them.

The D-9-5 allowance for funds earned in Korea

For D-9-5, up to KRW 50 million of the KRW 100 million investment can come from funds accumulated in Korea. The remainder must be foreign capital remitted from the home country (as of 2026). Typical examples include savings from part-time work, assistantships and scholarships during D-2 student or D-10 job-seeking status.

This allowance reduces the amount that must be remitted from abroad. Applicants must still document that the Korean funds were obtained lawfully.

Business expenditure and extensions of stay

Applicants must show that the remitted funds were spent on the business, rather than simply held in an account.

Contracts, tax invoices and other records must support each expense and show how the imported investment funds were used for the business.

D-9-4 and D-9-5 are initially granted for one year in most cases. Subsequent extension reviews examine revenue, tax filings, business premises and employment to confirm that the business is operating. Meeting the investment threshold at the initial application will not support an extension if there are no actual operations.

Domestic status changes and family visas

Foreign nationals already in Korea under statuses such as D-2, D-10, E-7 or F-3 can apply domestically to change to a D-9 category. This is the usual procedure for D-9-5 applicants who have Korean degrees and residence histories.

D-9-4 and D-9-5 holders may invite their spouse and minor children under F-3 dependent status. Their permitted stay is tied to the principal holder’s and ends when the principal’s stay ends. F-3 generally does not permit employment, so a spouse wishing to work must change to an appropriate status. D-9 status alone does not generally support an invitation for parents; a separate category must be considered.

Combining parental transfers and Korean savings

Consider an applicant who combines money sent to Korea by their parents with savings accumulated during studies in Korea, for a total of KRW 100 million. The applicant must document the parental gift and how the funds entered Korea from abroad. The Korean savings are subject to the KRW 50 million domestic-funds limit.

For the parental transfer, the applicant needs to establish the gift and ownership of the funds. For the savings, the questions are how they were earned and whether they exceed the allowance. Those sources and transfers must then be matched to planned business expenditure to assess which funds can be presented as qualifying investment.

Frequently asked questions

Does money my family sent count as investment?
Funds received as a family gift require evidence of the gift and the source of funds. Overseas remittance and other records must also establish that the funds entered Korea in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act.
If I meet the investment requirement, will extensions follow?
No. The extension review confirms actual operation of the business through revenue and tax filings, and extension becomes difficult without real operations.
