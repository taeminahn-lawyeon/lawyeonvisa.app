Reinstatement of Korean nationality after acquiring foreign citizenship

Former Korean nationals may reacquire Korean nationality through permission for reinstatement. The usual sequence is to report the loss of nationality, obtain F-4 status and complete domestic residence registration before applying for reinstatement. If a foreign citizenship certificate and the Korean family relations register contain different names or birth dates, the applicant must establish that both records refer to the same person.

Foreign citizenship and reporting the loss of Korean nationality

A Korean national who voluntarily acquires foreign nationality loses Korean nationality at the time of acquisition. The loss report records that event in Korea’s official records; the filing date does not change when nationality was lost.

Without a loss report, the family relations register and other Korean records continue to show the person as a Korean national. Acting as a Korean national, including using a Korean passport, can then create legal problems. The records must also reflect the loss of nationality to establish overseas Korean status for F-4 and reinstatement procedures.

F-4 status and domestic residence registration

F-4 status is available to former Korean nationals and their lineal descendants. Domestic residence registration is the registration procedure for long-term residence in Korea under F-4 status.

Applicants must maintain lawful immigration status during the reinstatement review. They therefore generally obtain F-4 status and complete domestic residence registration before applying.

Eligibility and review for nationality reinstatement

Applicants for reinstatement must prove that they previously held Korean nationality. This distinguishes them from applicants seeking Korean nationality for the first time through naturalization.

The reviewing authority may check identity, criminal records, military service records and immigration history. Permission cannot be granted to a person who has harmed the state or society, fails the good-conduct requirement, or lost or renounced nationality to evade military service. The authority reviews these matters in exercising its discretion to grant or refuse permission.

After permission for reinstatement is granted, the applicant acquires Korean nationality by taking the oath of allegiance and receiving the certificate of nationality reinstatement. The deadline to renounce foreign nationality is one year from the date Korean nationality is acquired. However, in certain cases prescribed by law — such as reinstatement for the purpose of permanent return after the age of 65 — a pledge not to exercise the foreign nationality may be made in place of renunciation, allowing dual nationality to be maintained.

Conflicting birth dates in foreign and Korean records

Both the loss report and the reinstatement application require confirmation that the foreign documents and the Korean register identify the same person. Where names differ, the applicant must explain the change and establish that both names refer to them.

If the date of birth on a US citizenship certificate differs from the Korean family relations register, the records conflict on a basic identifying fact. Recording an explanation on the prescribed form does not complete the proof of identity.

The first step is to establish which record will support correction of the other and whether correction must precede identity verification in Korea. If the foreign record needs to be corrected before a Korean filing can proceed, the F-4 and reinstatement timetable must allow for that correction. Filing the subsequent application too early can result in delay or rejection because the documents remain inconsistent.

Frequently asked questions

If I do not file the loss-of-nationality report, is my Korean nationality maintained?
Where foreign nationality was acquired voluntarily, Korean nationality is already lost at the moment of acquisition, regardless of whether the report is filed. Delaying the report does not preserve nationality; it only prolongs the state in which the records and the actual status diverge.
