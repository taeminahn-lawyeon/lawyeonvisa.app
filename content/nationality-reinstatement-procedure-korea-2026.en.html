Reinstatement of Korean nationality after acquiring foreign citizenship

Former Korean nationals may reacquire Korean nationality through nationality reinstatement. The application must establish their former Korean nationality, how it was lost, and that the current foreign passport holder is the person identified in the earlier Korean records.

Foreign citizenship and reporting nationality loss

A Korean national who voluntarily acquires foreign nationality loses Korean nationality at the time of acquisition. The loss report records that event in Korea’s official records; the filing date does not change when nationality was lost.

If the loss has not been recorded in Korea, a loss-of-nationality report should be submitted with evidence of when and how foreign citizenship was acquired. An entry in the family relations register does not preserve Korean nationality after voluntary acquisition of foreign nationality.

F-4 residence and applications through a Korean diplomatic mission

An overseas Korean planning to settle in Korea and apply for nationality reinstatement may reside in Korea with F-4 status if eligible. Long-term residence under F-4 status requires domestic residence registration. Lawful immigration status must be maintained throughout the reinstatement review in Korea.

A person with an address abroad may also apply through the Korean diplomatic mission responsible for that address. F-4 status and domestic residence registration are therefore not mandatory preliminary steps for every reinstatement application.

Eligibility and review for nationality reinstatement

The reviewing authority may check identity, criminal records, military service records and immigration history. Statutory grounds for refusal include having harmed the state or society, failing the good-conduct requirement, or having lost or renounced Korean nationality to evade military service.

Where an applicant has a criminal record, the assessment of the statutory good-conduct ground considers the nature and circumstances of the offense, the punishment, subsequent circumstances and the time elapsed. Likewise, failure to complete military service does not by itself establish an intention to evade service; the circumstances surrounding the loss or renunciation of nationality must be assessed.

Following approval, Korean nationality is generally acquired when the applicant takes the oath of allegiance and receives the nationality reinstatement certificate. Foreign nationality must be renounced within one year of acquiring Korean nationality.

Eligible applicants, including overseas Koreans aged 65 or older who return to settle permanently and apply in Korea, may instead pledge not to exercise foreign nationality in Korea. This is an exception to the renunciation requirement under Korean law. The pledge must also be made within one year of acquiring Korean nationality. Age 65 is not a minimum age for nationality reinstatement generally; younger applicants may also apply.

Different names or birth dates and proof of identity

Where the name in the Korean family relations register differs from the foreign passport, a name-change order or marriage certificate may establish how the name changed. If the evidence connects the former and current names to the same person, the foreign documents do not need to be corrected first simply to make the names match.

Where birth dates differ, evidence of the actual birth date and how each record was created must be examined. If the Korean family relations register is incorrect and evidence supports a correction, an application can be made to the family court for permission to correct that record. An error in a foreign record may require the relevant country’s correction procedure.

If one record places the applicant at 65 or older and another places them below 65, the age relevant to the dual-nationality exception must be established in addition to identity. Confirmation that both records concern the same person does not itself establish the correct date of birth.
