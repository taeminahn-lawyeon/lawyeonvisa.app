Employing E-7-4 and E-7-4R Workers Together: How the Employer Limit Applies

An employer with both E-7-4 skilled workers and E-7-4R regional skilled workers cannot calculate a separate allowance for each category and add the two together. Yet relying exclusively on the regional limit may also understate the available capacity where the general E-7-4 rule is more favorable.

The residence manual dated 1 September 2026 links employer recommendations to the relevant employment allowance. Applying the combined-employment rule and its exception correctly affects which workers an employer can plan to recommend, as well as the proposed route for their immigration status.

The two categories share an employer-level limit

For employers using both categories, the manual directs application of the regional employment allowance, with an exception where the general E-7-4 rule is more favorable. It also includes existing E-7-4 workers when calculating regional-category employment. The E-7-4R allowance is therefore not an additional allocation on top of existing E-7-4 employment.

The employer must first establish the relevant location, industry and qualifying Korean workforce, compare the applicable limits, and then account for workers in both categories. Separate calculations of unused places in each category can overstate the number of workers the employer can recommend.

When the general rule provides a higher limit

Consider a hypothetical business in a designated population-decline area, excluding construction. Assume it has 80 Korean employees who meet the counting requirements, 30 E-7-4 workers and 5 E-7-4R workers. This is an illustration of the rules, not a client matter or an approval result.

CalculationResult in this example
Regional ruleFor 51–100 Korean employees: up to 50%, capped at 35 → 35
General E-7-4 rule for this area50% of the qualifying Korean workforce → 40
Existing workers in both categories30 E-7-4 + 5 E-7-4R = 35
If the more favorable general rule appliesA total limit of 40, less the existing 35, leaves capacity for 5 under the headcount criterion

The employer cannot add the two limits to obtain 75 places. Conversely, applying only the regional cap of 35 would overlook the rule allowing the more favorable general standard. The remaining five places concern the headcount calculation only; they are not guaranteed recommendations or immigration approvals.

The 50% figure in this example is not a universal rule. The ordinary percentage, exceptions for designated areas and industries, and the construction calculation apply to different circumstances. An employer cannot select the most favorable number without first establishing that the business falls within its scope.

The payroll total may not be the qualifying headcount

The relevant Korean workforce is not necessarily everyone currently working at the business. General E-7-4 guidance uses Korean employees listed in employment insurance for at least three months. The regional guidance also refers to compliance with the minimum wage and at least three months of employment-insurance coverage. A newly hired employee who does not yet satisfy the counting requirements cannot immediately increase the allowance.

Foreign workers also need to be classified correctly. E-7-4 and E-7-4R workers count together for this limit, while other categories, including E-9, are excluded from that particular calculation. Insurance records and immigration-status records should reflect the same reference date before an employer determines how many recommendations it can support.

A more favorable limit does not remove regional obligations

Using the general E-7-4 employment allowance does not release an E-7-4R worker from the requirements of the regional route. These separately include a recommendation from the relevant metropolitan or provincial authority and an intention to reside and work in the recommended area for at least three years. An exception concerning employer headcount cannot be extended to the worker’s eligibility or regional obligations.

An employer planning to retain experienced workers should establish its applicable total limit and existing headcount, then assess which workers satisfy the relevant status requirements within that limit. Local-government recommendation arrangements, employment contracts and individual points remain separate considerations. The manual provides a rule and exception for combined employment; it does not permit employers to assemble a new visa route from whichever conditions of the two systems they prefer.
