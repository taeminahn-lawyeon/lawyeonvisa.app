D-9 extensions and changes to F-2-99 residence status

D-9-4 and D-9-5 are initially granted for one year in most cases. Extensions depend on a review of the business’s operations. After at least five years under D-9 status, the holder may apply to change to F-2-99 residence status. If approved, the holder can continue the same business with a more stable basis for long-term residence.

Business operations in a D-9 extension review

A D-9 extension review examines revenue shown in tax filings, evidence that the business premises are maintained, the applicant’s residence and employment records. Meeting the initial investment requirement will not support an extension if the business is not actually operating.

The review covers both the position at the time of application and the operating record. Retaining sales, tax and business records allows the applicant to demonstrate continued operations throughout the period of stay.

F-2-99 eligibility requirements

In addition to at least five years under D-9 status, an F-2-99 application is assessed against residence, conduct, income and assets, and language and integration requirements.

F-2-99 conversion review areas (as of 2026)

Detailed criteria may change and should be reconfirmed at the time of application.

The conduct assessment takes account of immigration violations, criminal penalties and tax arrears during the applicant’s stay. The applicant must also have the required income and asset evidence and language qualifications when applying. These requirements may not all be satisfied on the date the five-year residence period is completed.

Family residence and life in Korea

F-3 dependent family members must each obtain an extension before their own permitted stay expires. Applications are assessed by reference to the principal holder’s permitted stay and the family relationship. F-3 generally does not permit employment. A spouse wishing to work or operate a separate business must qualify for an appropriate status based on their education and experience.

School-age children can attend Korean elementary, middle and high schools. Universities also offer special admission tracks for foreign applicants whose parents are both foreign nationals.

Foreign nationals eligible for self-employed National Health Insurance generally become insured after six months in Korea. Workplace coverage, dependent registration and the start of coverage for particular immigration statuses follow separate rules. Real-estate purchases face few legal restrictions, and the basic tax framework is the same as for Korean nationals. Commercial tenants’ rights to request lease renewal and recover a business premium also apply regardless of nationality. Residence-based tax concessions, such as the one-household-one-home exemption, and certain commercial lease provisions above the statutory converted-deposit threshold may apply differently.

Income, violation records and application timing

Consider a D-9 holder who has completed five years in Korea but whose previous annual business income fluctuates around the threshold and who has one immigration penalty on record. The outcome depends on the assessed income and the nature of the violation as well as the length of residence.

Annual income records, the violation and elapsed time, and the date the language requirement was met identify any outstanding requirements. Application timing also affects the period assessed. The appropriate filing date therefore depends on when the requirements are met, rather than simply the five-year anniversary.

Frequently asked questions

After five years, does F-2-99 follow automatically?
No. Immigration also assesses residence, conduct, income and language requirements before deciding an F-2-99 application.
Can my spouse work in Korea?
F-3 dependent status generally does not permit employment. A spouse wishing to work or run a business must qualify for an appropriate status based on their education and experience.
