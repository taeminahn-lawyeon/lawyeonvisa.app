D-10-2 start-up preparation and D-8-4 visa requirements

Foreign nationals with a bachelor’s degree or higher, or a Korean associate degree, may apply for D-10-2 status to prepare a technology start-up. The permitted stay depends on the qualifying ground, such as holding or applying for intellectual property rights, or participating in or completing OASIS training. Those planning to change to D-8-4 must use the preparation period to meet its mandatory and total-points requirements.

D-10-2 eligibility

D-10-2 is available to holders of a bachelor’s degree or higher, or a Korean associate degree, who meet one of the following criteria (as of 2026): 1) ownership of a Korean patent, utility model or design right, or a pending application for which examination has been requested; 2) current participation in an OASIS course run by a Global Start-up Immigration Center jointly designated by the Ministry of Justice and the Ministry of SMEs and Startups, or completion within the past three years; 3) participation in the K-Startup Grand Challenge with a recommendation from the head of the Korea Institute of Startup and Entrepreneurship Development; or 4) ownership of intellectual property rights in an overseas OECD country. An OASIS course completed more than three years ago does not qualify unless the applicant is retaking it.

Common disqualification grounds include a sentence of imprisonment or more severe punishment within five years before applying, a criminal fine of KRW 3 million or more within three years, or immigration penalties meeting the stay-restriction thresholds.

Start-up preparation activities and stay periods

D-10-2 permits immigration training for entrepreneurs, preparing and filing patent and other IP applications, and preparations to incorporate a company. Internships are restricted, and the part-time work exception for D-10-1 holders does not apply. Applicants planning to fund their preparation period through internships or part-time work in Korea will therefore need another source of living expenses.

A stay of one year per grant, up to three years in total, is available to applicants with at least 35 start-up immigration points, recommended K-Startup Grand Challenge participants, holders of Korean patent, utility model or design rights, and holders of IP rights in overseas OECD countries. Applicants with pending IP applications, OASIS participants and graduates, and those meeting at least one mandatory points item may receive six months per grant, up to one year in total (as of 2026).

Points required for D-8-4

D-10-2 covers preparation before operating a business. A change to D-8-4 requires at least one mandatory item and a total of 60 out of 300 points under the start-up immigration points system (as of 2026).

Mandatory items include 1) holding IP rights (60 points for a patent; 30 for a utility model or design) or being the inventor of registered IP (30 points for a patent, for example); 2) receiving government start-up support or completing an advanced OASIS course (30 points); 3) at least three years under E-1 or E-3 status (30 points); or 4) attracting investment of at least KRW 100 million (60 points). Optional points are available for individual OASIS courses, Korean proficiency and a master’s degree or higher. Patent registration, OASIS completion and investment secured during D-10-2 stay can count towards the D-8-4 score. Each planned activity can therefore be assessed for whether it meets a mandatory item or adds to the total.

Graduates of Korean universities with a master’s degree or higher, or a bachelor’s degree and OASIS points, may also consider the D-9-5 sole-proprietor route. The requirements to examine depend on whether the intended business will operate as a technology start-up company under D-8-4 or a sole proprietorship under D-9-5.

Stay periods for pending and registered patents

An OASIS participant with a pending patent application and an expiring D-2 stay could apply for D-10-2 on the pending-application ground, receiving six months per grant up to one year. Patent registration or a score of 35 would instead allow the one-year-per-grant category, up to three years. Waiting for registration to secure a longer preparation period may, however, extend beyond the current stay. The expected registration timeline and the stay expiry date therefore affect when the application can be made.

The patent examination stage and OASIS completion date establish which qualifying grounds the applicant can currently prove. The application may need to rely on the pending patent, or there may be time to secure registration or 35 points before the current stay expires. Those dates determine the available application ground and expected stay period. The applicant must also check whether an OASIS course was completed within the preceding three years.

OASIS completion and the start-up timetable

The start-up timetable must include both the D-10-2 application and the date by which a mandatory D-8-4 item can be met. Applying for D-10-2 before the current stay expires secures only the preparation stage. The later change depends on completing the patent registration, OASIS training or investment needed for a mandatory item and 60 points within the available preparation period.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work part-time or intern on D-10-2?
No. The scope of D-10-2 activity is start-up preparation; internships are restricted and the part-time work concession available under general job seeker (D-10-1) status does not apply.
If I qualify, does the start-up visa follow?
A change to D-8-4 requires a separate assessment, including at least one mandatory item and 60 start-up immigration points. Patent registration, OASIS training and investment during the D-10-2 period can contribute to that score.
