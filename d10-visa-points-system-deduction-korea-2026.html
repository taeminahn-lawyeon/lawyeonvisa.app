D-10-1 job seeker visa points and deductions

Category Visa & ImmigrationReading time approx. 7 min

The D-10-1 system requires at least 20 basic points for age and education and a total of 60 out of 190 points, including optional and bonus points (as of 2026). Applicants with criminal fines or immigration penalties in the past five years must meet those thresholds after deductions. A sentence of imprisonment or certain fines and penalties disqualify an applicant regardless of the score.

Basic, optional and bonus points

The system allocates up to 50 basic points, 70 optional points and 70 bonus points (as of 2026).

Applicants aged 30–34 receive the highest age score, at 20 points. Education, supported by degree certificates, receives 15 points for a Korean associate degree or a bachelor’s degree, 20 for a master’s degree and 30 for a doctorate. Study in Korea receives 30 points within three years of graduation regardless of degree level, with a lower score afterwards. TOPIK level or KIIP stage earns 5 to 20 points. The leading-university bonus applies to those who graduated within the last three years from a university in the THE top 200 or QS top 500.

Nationals of countries designated by the Minister of Justice who hold certain statuses, including C-3 or G-1, need a total of at least 80 points to change status.

Fine and penalty deductions and disqualification

Deductions cover dispositions within five years before the application date. Immigration penalties deduct 30 points for KRW 3 million or more, 10 points for KRW 1 million to under 3 million, and 5 points for KRW 500,000 to under 1 million. Criminal fines deduct 30 points for KRW 3 million or more, 10 points for KRW 2 million to under 3 million, and 5 points for amounts under KRW 2 million. Deductions from both categories are added together. Administrative fines are excluded (as of 2026).

An application is barred regardless of score for: 1) a sentence of imprisonment or more severe punishment within five years before the application; 2) a deportation or departure order under the Immigration Control Act within five years; 3) a criminal fine of KRW 3 million or more within three years; or 4) immigration penalties meeting the stay-restriction threshold of KRW 5 million or more for a first offence or KRW 7 million or more in total within three years (as of 2026). A separate article addresses the wider immigration effects of criminal fines and immigration penalties.

If a pending criminal case results in a fine, its amount and the application date determine whether a deduction or a disqualification rule applies. For example, a fine of KRW 3 million or more within three years before applying bars the application; earning additional points cannot overcome that bar.

Stay periods and language-certificate validity

A score of at least 80 allows one year per grant, up to three years in total. A score of 60 to 79 allows six months per grant, up to one year in total (as of 2026). Applicants who need more than a year to find employment must meet the 80-point threshold.

Completion of KIIP stages and passes in the interim and comprehensive evaluations remain valid without a time limit. Pre-evaluation scores are valid for two years from the announcement of results, and TOPIK results remain valid only until the expiry date on the certificate.

Deduction periods and application timing

An applicant with 65 basic, optional and bonus points and a KRW 2.5 million fine from four years ago loses 10 points, leaving 55 and falling below the threshold. The deduction ends when that disposition falls outside the five-year period before the application date. Additional language or work-experience points could allow the applicant to reach the threshold before those five years have elapsed.

Waiting for a deduction to expire depends on having permission to stay until then. Earning additional points instead requires obtaining the language or work-experience evidence before the current stay expires. Comparing the disposition date, the stay expiry date and the earliest date for obtaining additional points establishes whether waiting is feasible or the score must be improved sooner.

Scores and evidence at the application date

The score must be supported by evidence valid on the application date. While an applicant waits for a deduction to expire, a TOPIK certificate may expire or the study-in-Korea score may fall after three years from graduation. The final score may therefore remain lower than expected even after the deduction ends. The 20-point basic minimum, the total score and any remaining disqualification grounds must all be checked against the same application date.

Frequently asked questions

If I pass 60 points, can I stay for three years?
No. Between 60 and 79 points, stay is limited to six months per grant and one year in total; stay of one year per grant up to three years requires a total of 80 points or more (as of 2026).
Does a fine record rule out the job seeker visa?
It depends on the amount and timing. Fines and penalties within five years of the application count as deductions, while records above the thresholds — such as a criminal fine of KRW 3M or more within three years — are disqualification grounds that bar the application (as of 2026).
