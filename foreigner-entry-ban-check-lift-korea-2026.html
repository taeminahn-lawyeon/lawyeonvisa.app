Entry-Ban Relief and Re-entry After a Departure Order or Deportation

Category Immigration Violations · CriminalReading time approx. 7 min

Returning to family life or business in Korea after a departure order or deportation can involve both entry-ban relief and the procedures for actual admission. For someone still in Korea, challenging the obligation to leave may also remain necessary. The work depends on the current decisions and whether departure has occurred.

Lifting an entry ban resolves one part of the problem of returning to Korea. It does not itself authorize residence or guarantee a visa or admission. Removal, restrictions on returning and the basis for activities after entry therefore need to be addressed together.

The obligation to leave and the entry ban have different effects

A departure order requires a person to leave by a specified deadline; a deportation order provides the basis for enforced removal. An entry ban concerns subsequent admission to Korea. A removal measure may be accompanied by an entry ban, but an exemption from the ban can also be granted while the departure order remains.

The fact that a departure order was issued does not establish whether a ban exists or how long it lasts. The decisions, grounds and period of restriction, and any subsequent cancellation, amendment or lifting of the ban need to be distinguished. A visa refusal alone also does not establish an entry ban for a particular period.

If existing circumstances were misunderstood or the wrong criteria were applied, a challenge to the original decision may arise. Seeking relief because entry is necessary now, despite a remaining ban period, is a different request. The issues can be connected, but they are not always the same procedure or mandatory successive steps.

Challenging a departure or deportation order

The Immigration Control Act objection against a deportation order must be filed within seven days of receiving it. The same objection does not apply to a departure order. It is also incorrect to state that this objection must precede every lawsuit challenging removal.

The general time limits for a revocation action are 90 days from learning of the decision and one year from the decision. Separate rules govern matters such as the starting date following an administrative appeal, so the applicable period depends on the decision and procedural history.

Filing a lawsuit does not automatically suspend the decision or its enforcement. Where departure or enforcement is imminent, the grounds for revocation and the requirements for suspension both matter. An official saying that a matter will be reviewed, or receipt of a request to lift a ban, does not itself change the original decision.

A challenge addresses errors in the applicable criteria or facts, and the exercise of administrative discretion. It involves explaining the legal significance of family circumstances and economic harm in the particular decision, rather than merely listing those circumstances.

Ordinary and special lifting of a ban after departure

Ordinary lifting following expiry of the ban period is distinct from special lifting sought on national-interest or humanitarian grounds while the period remains. Even after expiry, other grounds for exclusion and the requirements for admission may still apply.

Family life with a spouse or children in Korea, care of minor children, treatment needed in Korea, and maintaining business operations and employment may support a need for entry. Their existence alone does not guarantee relief.

A ground for special relief does not necessarily have to arise after departure. An earlier circumstance may still support a present need to enter. If the same circumstance was considered in the original decision, the application needs to explain why entry is now justified despite that assessment.

Connecting family, treatment and business circumstances to a present need

The existence of a family relationship is distinct from an actual need for care or support. Where a family member is ill, the severity and care arrangements affect why the applicant needs to enter. An applicant’s past treatment likewise does not establish a current need for treatment in Korea.

For a business, incorporation or registration is distinct from the reason the applicant’s presence is needed. Actual operations, employment relationships and the applicant’s role are matters to connect to the claimed need for entry.

These grounds are considered alongside the nature and repetition of past offences and events since the decision. A suspended sentence reflecting a settlement or remorse in the criminal case does not itself substitute for grounds for entry-ban exemption or relief.

Re-entry after relief and instructing Lawyeon

Lifting a ban does not automatically restore a previous residence status. Where a visa is required, its requirements are considered in relation to the purpose of entry and intended activities. Admission inspection remains necessary even after a visa is issued.

Resuming family life and personally operating a Korean business involve different activities after entry. The purpose advanced in the relief application needs to connect with the activities presented in subsequent visa and entry procedures.
